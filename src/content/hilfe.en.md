# Help

## What is a storyboard?

A storyboard is a plan for your media product — for example a film, a photo
story, an audio play or a podcast.
You break your idea down into individual scenes and think about each one:
What do you see? What do you hear? What does the team need to know?

With a good storyboard, everyone knows what to do during the shoot.

## The workflow in five steps

The progress bar at the top shows where you are:
**Format → Set up → Storyboard → Review → Export.**

1. **Choose a format** — first choose video, image, audio or stage, then tap your media product. “Your own idea” sits below the format groups. **Load example** starts a filled-in example project instead (this replaces an open project; the app asks first).
2. **Set up** — enter a project name (required). New projects start at **Simple**. Standard and Pro are available under **More details** when needed.
3. **Storyboard** — this is where you plan and add scenes (see below). Above the storyboard there is a collapsible **Planning** section:
   - **Logline:** What is it about? Describe your idea in one sentence.
   - **Objective:** What should your audience understand or feel?
   - **Roles:** Who takes on which task (camera, editing, speaker …)?
   - **Resources:** What do you need? Locations, equipment, props.
4. **Review** — the app lists every scene with its status (Empty, Partial, Complete). **Edit** jumps straight to the scene so you can fill it in.
5. **Export** — save your project, create a PDF, print or start the presentation.

## Scenes

- **Add a scene:** Click “+ Add scene” at the bottom.
- **Insert an image:** Tap the dashed square of a scene. You can take a photo or choose a file (PNG or JPEG). The image is resized automatically.
- **Image description (alt text):** Once an image is there you can describe it briefly. This helps people who use the app with a screen reader.
- **Fill in the text:**
  - **Action / image description:** What happens in this scene?
  - **Text and sound:** The label matches your format, for example voiceover, speech bubble, dialogue or speaker notes.
  - **More fields:** The app only shows fields that fit the format and level of detail. Camera or material fields appear when they are relevant.
- **Reorder:** Hold the dots icon of a scene and drag it to the desired position.
- **Duplicate:** The copy icon creates a copy of the scene right below it.
- **Delete:** The trash icon deletes the scene. If that was a mistake: an “Undo” appears briefly at the bottom — tap it quickly.

## Undo and redo

At the top you find the **Undo** and **Redo** arrows. They cover your texts,
scenes, images and settings. The shortcuts Ctrl/Cmd + Z and
Ctrl/Cmd + Shift + Z also work as long as you are not typing in a text field
(there the normal text undo applies).

## Comments

The **Comments** switch at the top shows a comment field on every scene. Teachers
or classmates can use it to give feedback. Comments are saved in the
`.storyboard` file and can be ticked off. They do not appear in print or PDF.

## Additional fields and format templates

Using **Configure fields** next to the storyboard heading you can add, rename
or delete up to 20 additional fields for all scenes. Deleting a field also
removes its entries from all scenes.

Every format comes with a matching field template. Use “Add” in the dialog to
restore missing template fields. Your own fields and any values already entered
are kept. When you change format, unrelated template fields are hidden rather
than deleted.

## Saving and loading

Your browser remembers your work automatically on **this device**.
That is not enough if you switch devices or share the device with others!

- **Save locally:** Open the **File** menu at the top and choose “Save” (or use the export step). You receive a `.storyboard` file. Keep it safe (e.g. school cloud, USB stick, email it to yourself).
- **Load a project:** Open **File** → “Load” and choose your `.storyboard` file. Note: this replaces the currently open project; the app asks first.
- **Reset:** **File** → “Reset” deletes the current project and the automatic backup on this device. Save a file first if you still need it.

**Tip:** Save a file at the end of every work session. The file is your backup.

## Exporting as PDF and printing

- **Download PDF:** Click “PDF” at the top. The app creates a `.pdf` file directly in the browser and downloads it.
- **Print:** Click “Print” at the top. This opens your device’s print dialog.

On iPad, browser downloads can vary depending on school device or MDM settings. If the direct PDF download is blocked, use “Print” and save as PDF from the print dialog.

## Presenting

**Present** shows your storyboard full screen, scene by scene, for example on a
projector. Use the arrow keys (or the space bar for “next”) to change scenes, the
**Play** button to advance automatically, and Escape to leave the presentation.

## Display and language

- **Language:** At the top you switch between German, English, Spanish and French. The Help, For teachers and About pages exist only in German and English, Privacy and Imprint only in German.
- **Display and text size:** In the footer you choose Light, Dark or High contrast and the text size. The app remembers both on this device.

## Frequently asked questions

**My work is gone!**
Did you switch devices or browsers? The automatic backup only applies to one device and one browser. Load your most recently saved `.storyboard` file.

**Can anyone see my data?**
No. Everything stays on your device. Nothing is uploaded to the internet.

**How many scenes can I create?**
Up to 200. For a short film, 8–15 scenes are usually enough.
